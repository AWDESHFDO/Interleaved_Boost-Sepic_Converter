The project focuses on the design, simulation, and implementation of an Interleaved Boost–SEPIC Converter to achieve high power density, reduced current ripple, and improved efficiency. The converter combines the advantages of both Boost and SEPIC topologies, allowing step-up/step-down voltage capability with continuous input current. The interleaved structure helps distribute the load between multiple phases, minimizing stress on individual switches and components. Simulation studies were carried out using MATLAB/Simulink to analyze current sharing, voltage regulation, and switching performance. A hardware prototype was then developed to validate the design, using a Wavect controller for PWM generation and a custom driver board fabricated in Altium Designer. Measurements using a power analyzer and oscilloscope were performed to study efficiency, waveform characteristics, and harmonic distortion. Experimental results closely matched simulation outcomes, confirming that the Interleaved Boost–SEPIC Converter provides better dynamic response, lower ripple, and higher efficiency compared to conventional single-phase designs.
